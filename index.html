Overview

What is Tangentgarden?

Tangentgarden is a cozy, typing-driven farming and progression game. It blends the calm of a garden sim with the satisfying, measurable improvement of a typing trainer — and turns both into something you can watch grow.

At its heart, Tangentgarden is a game about two simple pleasures: getting better at typing, and tending a garden. You sit down, open the game, and start typing words. Each correctly typed word does something tangible in the world — it plants a seed, waters a row, speeds a crop toward harvest, or earns a little coin. Your keyboard becomes a watering can, a trowel, and a pair of pruning shears all at once.

Unlike a strict typing tutor, there is no harsh red error tone and no countdown shouting at you. Mistakes are gentle. You correct, you continue, and your garden keeps growing. The game rewards accuracy and consistency, but it never punishes you for being slow. Whether you type at thirty words a minute or one hundred and twenty, the garden responds to you, not to some leaderboard.

As you type, crops ripen and can be harvested for produce. Produce can be sold at the market for coins, and coins unlock new seeds, decorations, garden expansions, and cosmetic flourishes. Over time, a bare patch of dirt becomes a lush, personalized garden that reflects both how much you've played and how much your typing has improved. That dual progression — skill and scenery — is what makes Tangentgarden feel different from either a typing app or a farming game on its own.

Everything is built around long, relaxed sessions. There are no energy bars, no daily login walls, and no ads. You can play for ten minutes between meetings or lose an evening to a marathon harvest. The game saves your garden, your collections, and your typing statistics locally. An optional account uploads selected statistics; farm export/import moves your whole garden.

The world is deliberately cozy and colorful. Crops are grouped into themed collections — root vegetables, herbs, berries, flowers, and more — and each new discovery is logged in a journal you can browse like a small encyclopedia. Completing a collection unlocks rewards, decorative items, and sometimes a whole new set of seeds to chase.

Tangentgarden is made by a small indie team and is free to download. It runs offline on Windows, reads keyboard state even while you work in other apps, and never sends what you type anywhere. If you've ever wanted a reason to practice typing that wasn't a blank screen and a blinking cursor, this is it.

How it plays

The core gameplay loop

Tangentgarden runs on a single, satisfying loop. Each step feeds the next, and the whole cycle is powered by your typing.

  1. 1

    Type words

    The game presents words drawn from your current crop's seed set. Type them correctly to channel energy into the garden.

  2. 2

    Grow crops

    Each correct word advances a crop's growth stage. Accuracy and speed shorten the time to harvest.

  3. 3

    Harvest items

    Ripe crops can be harvested into produce — carrots, tulips, herbs, and rarer finds as you unlock higher tiers.

  4. 4

    Sell produce

    Take harvests to the market. Daily orders pay a premium, so you decide between steady income and chasing a specific request.

  5. 5

    Earn currency

    Coins from sales fund everything downstream — new seeds, decorations, expansions, and cosmetic flourishes.

  6. 6

    Unlock content

    Spend coins to unlock new crop families, garden plots, decorative items, and collection sets you haven't seen yet.

  7. 7

    Expand garden

    New plots and decorations let you lay out a larger, more personal garden. Form starts to matter as much as function.

  8. 8

    Complete collections

    Discovering every crop in a set fills its journal page and grants a completion reward — often a unique decoration.

  9. 9

    Earn achievements

    Milestones for typing, harvesting, and collecting grant badges and sometimes unlock new content tiers.

  10. ↻

    Repeat — and improve

    Every loop makes you a slightly faster, slightly more accurate typist, and leaves your garden a little fuller than before.

Core mechanic

The typing system

This is the engine of the whole game. It's designed to feel calm, fair, and rewarding — whether you're a beginner or already fast.

The typing system is a set of lightweight challenges rather than one rigid test. Words appear in themed bundles tied to what you're growing — root vegetables pull from a root-vegetable word set, herbs from an herb set — so the language you type stays connected to what's happening on screen.

The game measures accuracy (correct characters over total typed) and words per minute (WPM) on every challenge. Both feed a session score and your long-term statistics. A gentle combo system rewards streaks of correct characters with a small growth bonus, so consistent typing feels visibly more productive than start-and-stop typing.

As your average WPM and accuracy rise, the game unlocks new typing tiers. Higher tiers introduce longer words, richer crop sets, and bigger combo multipliers — but only if your rolling average is ready for them. You can't brute-force content by grinding; you unlock it by genuinely improving.

Milestones act as friendly checkpoints: 30 WPM, 45 WPM, 60 WPM, 80 WPM, and 100+ WPM each grant a reward and a small ceremony in the garden. Hitting one for the first time plants a commemorative tree you can place anywhere.

Discovery

The journal system

Every crop you've ever grown is recorded in an in-game journal that doubles as a small encyclopedia and a completion checklist.

Crop encyclopedia Herbarium collection

The journal is your record of everything you've encountered. Each crop gets a page with its name, a short description, the collection it belongs to, and a small illustration. Pages start blank and fill in as you discover and harvest each crop for the first time.

Collection tracking shows how many of a set you've found and what's still missing, so you always have a clear next target. Crop encyclopedia entries include growing notes — how fast the crop ripens, what typing tier it needs, and what it sells for.

Discovery progress is a global percentage across every collection, while unlock tracking lists the typing milestones and coin goals that gate remaining content. Statistics inside the journal tie each crop to how many times you've grown and harvested it, and completion goals surface the last few items standing between you and a finished set.

Economy

The market system

The market turns your harvests into the currency that funds the rest of the game. It's simple, but it gives every session a shape.

Selling crops is the baseline: drop produce into the market and receive coins based on rarity and typing tier. Daily orders ask for specific bundles — say, six carrots and three basil — and pay a premium, so each session has an optional goal to aim for.

Managing resources means deciding what to grow next based on both what orders want and what your typing can comfortably handle. Higher-tier crops sell for more but require higher WPM to grow efficiently.

Orders refresh on a gentle timer and stack, so you're never punished for stepping away. Progression rewards from completing order streaks grant bonus coins and occasional exclusive decorations, and the economy systems are tuned to reward consistent, accurate typing over raw speed alone.

Persistence

The account system

You can play forever without an account. If you do make one, it's to keep your garden safe across computers — nothing more.

Farm summary Long-term garden projects

Your player profile holds your garden, collections, achievements, and typing statistics in one place. It's local first: everything is saved on your machine, and the game is fully playable offline with no account.

Creating an optional account enables long-term progression sync. Move to a new laptop, reinstall your OS, or play on a second machine, and your garden comes with you. The account stores your progression snapshot — not your typing keystrokes — and sync is encrypted in transit.

Statistics like lifetime WPM, accuracy, crops grown, and collections completed live on the profile so you can see the full arc of your improvement. Tracking progress across sessions is the point of the account: a multi-week view of a skill getting sharper, attached to a garden that kept getting prettier along the way.

🛡️

Accounts are optional. No account is needed to download, install, or play the game, and an account never stores what you type — only the aggregate statistics the game derives from it.

Your canvas

The garden system

The garden is where everything you've earned becomes a place. This is the most personal part of Tangentgarden — and it's large on purpose.

Your garden starts as a modest plot of tilled soil with a few usable squares. As you harvest, sell, and unlock, it grows into a sprawling, fully customizable space. You decide what grows where, what decorates the edges, and how the whole thing looks when you step back. The garden is equal parts farm and canvas: function (crops you can harvest) and form (decorations you can place) share the same grid, and the best gardens balance both.

Building your garden is a grid-based affair. Each unlocked plot tile can hold a crop or a decoration. Crops produce; decorations make it yours. You can pick up and move almost anything at any time, with no penalty, so rearranging is encouraged rather than costly. Over sessions, bare dirt becomes terraced rows, herb spirals, flower borders, and quiet little reading nooks.

Decorations number over two hundred and range from the practical — fences, paths, trellises, lamps — to the purely delightful — birdbaths, wind chimes, benches, seasonal banners. Many are unlocked through collections and achievements, so a decoration is often also a memento of something you accomplished.

Layout customization includes pathing, elevation tiers, and themed zones. Want a root-vegetable field in one corner and a wildflower meadow in another? You can. Unlockables expand the grid itself, add new biomes (a greenhouse, a moonlit grove), and introduce seasonal palettes that recolor the whole garden.

Visual progression is dramatic. A new garden looks humble; a hundred-hour garden looks like a postcard. And because personal expression is the reward for the typing that funded it, no two endgame gardens ever look the same. Yours becomes a record of what you grew, what you got better at, and what you decided was beautiful.

The point of it all

Why typing matters

Typing is not a gimmick bolted onto a farming skin. It is the core progression system, and everything in Tangentgarden grows from your skill getting better.

In most games, your character "levels up" because you spent time. In Tangentgarden, you level up — for real, measurably — and the game reflects that back at you. The garden is the mirror. When your accuracy climbs, crops ripen faster and orders fill sooner. When your WPM crosses a threshold, a new tier of seeds unlocks. The progression isn't a number on a screen; it's a skill you keep even after you close the game.

That matters because typing is one of the few in-game skills that transfers directly to the rest of your life. The hour you spend growing a virtual herb garden also makes you faster at writing email, coding, taking notes, and chatting. The game has the cozy, low-stakes shape of a farming sim, but underneath it is a genuinely useful typing trainer — and it never asks you to choose between "doing something useful" and "doing something fun."

Privacy & trust

Tangentgarden reads keyboard input because typing is the game. That understandably raises a question — "is this a keylogger?" — so here's a direct, plain-language answer.

🛡️

Tangentgarden is not a keylogger.

Key states grow your garden. Your typed text is not recorded. While running, Tangentgarden can detect key presses even when another app has focus. It uses them for gameplay and keeps counters, not a transcript of what you write.

🖥️

Works alongside other apps

Work mode lets your garden grow while you type elsewhere. Windows key states are sampled while the game runs; the game does not need keyboard focus.

🚫

No typed-text history

The ordinary input path checks which keys are down. It does not turn those samples into sentences, read documents, or keep an ordered log of your typing.

🔍

Inspect the input code

See real source excerpts below, with complete input files to read. The distinction is visible in the implementation: temporary key states and counters, rather than text recording.

🔑

Pause when you need to

Pause stops gameplay registration. You can also choose apps that automatically pause registration. These controls do not stop the input sampler; exit the game to stop sampling.

🔒

Local counters & saves

Your farm saves include daily and lifetime counts per key. These are totals, not a sequence of presses. The separate typing trial uses text entered into its own practice field.

🎮

Account optional

Play offline without signing in. Optional account features use email and a password for authentication; statistics uploads send progression totals, not raw input samples or typed text.

A key state, not a character

The high bit answers one question: is this virtual key down right now? This function returns a boolean, not typed text. It works across apps, so focus is not a privacy boundary.

private static bool IsDown(int key)
{
    // Den högsta biten visar om knappen är nere just nu.
    return (GetAsyncKeyState(key) & 0x8000) != 0;
}

The worker samples roughly every 10 ms. The main thread takes samples out of the queue for gameplay. At 256 pending samples the queue is cleared, limiting the temporary backlog; it is not a saved typing history.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

The questions we expect most — especially the ones about trust and what the game does with your keyboard.

Is Tangentgarden a keylogger?

Tangentgarden samples key states across apps for gameplay, but does not keep a transcript or an ordered keystroke log. Local saves include per-key counters. Inspect the input code above for the implementation and its limits.

Does the game read what I type in other apps?

It detects key presses even while other apps have focus, so Work mode can grow your garden. The ordinary input path samples Windows key states rather than reading the text inside those apps. Pause or app exclusions stop gameplay registration; exit the game to stop sampling.

Why does the game need keyboard input at all?

Because typing is the core gameplay mechanic. Words you type grow crops, fill orders, and earn currency. The keyboard is the controller — there's no other input method because there's no other way to play a typing game.

Does it collect passwords or personal information?

The ordinary input path does not reconstruct or save passwords or typed sentences. The optional account does ask for an email and password to authenticate with the account service over HTTPS. Login passwords and session tokens are not saved to disk in this version. Play offline without an account if you prefer.

Do I need an account to play?

No. The game is fully playable offline with no account. An optional account uploads selected progression statistics, not the full farm or raw key samples. Use the game’s explicit farm export/import to move the whole farm to another computer.

Is the game really free?

Yes. Tangentgarden is free to download and play, with no ads, no microtransactions, and no energy systems. It's an indie project made by a small team.

What platforms does it support?

Currently Windows PC. We'd like to bring it to other desktop platforms in the future, but for now it's built and tested for Windows.

Do I need an internet connection?

No. You can play the entire game offline. The only feature that uses the network is optional account sync, which you can ignore entirely.

How does typing actually affect the game?

Correct words advance crop growth, build combos that speed things up, and contribute to your WPM and accuracy statistics. Higher WPM and accuracy unlock new crop tiers, so genuine skill improvement opens new content.

What if I'm a slow typist?

That's perfectly fine. The game scales to your speed, rewards accuracy and consistency over raw speed, and never punishes you for being slow. Many players start below 40 WPM and improve naturally over time.

How long is the game?

There's no fixed end. Completing every collection and achievement can take dozens to hundreds of hours depending on your pace, and the garden is endlessly customizable. It's designed for long, relaxed play over weeks and months.

Can I play on multiple computers?

Yes, with an optional account. Your garden, collections, achievements, and statistics sync across machines so a new computer never means a lost garden. Without an account, your save stays on the machine you played on.

What are the system requirements?

The game is lightweight. It runs on any modest Windows PC from the last several years — see the Download section for the specifics.

Is there controller or mouse support?

Typing is done with the keyboard by design. The mouse is used for menus, placing decorations, and navigating the journal and stats — but the core gameplay is keyboard typing.

Where can I download it?

From the Download section on this page, which links to GitHub releases. It's a simple installer and the game runs offline immediately after install.

How do I report a bug or give feedback?

Through the GitHub repository or our Discord — both linked in the footer. We're a small team but we read everything.
